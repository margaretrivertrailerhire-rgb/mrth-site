Sand and soil are heavy

This is the one thing that catches people out. Mulch is light and bulky, so a trailer load is limited by space. Sand and soil are the opposite — a cubic metre of damp sand weighs somewhere around 1.5 tonnes, so a trailer load is limited by weight, not volume.

In practice that means a trailer tops out near 1.5–1.6 m³ of sand, not the 5.5 m³ you'd get in mulch. If you need more than about two cubic metres, a truck load is almost always the cheaper way to get it — one drop instead of four trips.

How much do I need?

Depths that actually work

Soil and sand jobs vary far more than mulch, because the right depth depends entirely on what you're doing:

  • Lawn topdressing — 10 to 20 mm. One cubic metre covers about 100 m² at 10 mm. Go thicker than 20 mm in a single pass and you'll smother the grass rather than level it.
  • Turf underlay — 100 mm. About 10 m² per cubic metre. Enough for roots to establish before they hit whatever's underneath.
  • Raised vegie beds — 300 mm or more. A 3 m × 1 m bed at 300 mm takes just under a cubic metre.
  • Garden bed top-up — 50 mm. Around 20 m² per cubic metre.
  • Paving and brick paving bedding sand — 30 mm. Roughly 33 m² per cubic metre, before compaction.

The calculator on the homepage will do the arithmetic from your measurements if you'd rather not.

Which one

Choosing a soil or sand

Soils

Vegie / Garden Blend ($130/m³) — the one for productive beds. Rich, dark and ready to plant into, so it suits raised vegie boxes and new ornamental beds where you want growth this season rather than next.

Soil Conditioner ($125/m³) — not a standalone growing medium. This is what you dig through tired existing soil to bring structure and organic matter back into it. Margaret River's sandier blocks benefit from it most.

Landscape Blend ($125/m³) — general-purpose bulk fill for shaping beds, building up levels and backfilling. Cheaper than a garden blend and perfectly good where it isn't the top 100 mm.

Lawn Blend ($95/m³) — sandier and free-draining, formulated to level and topdress lawn without holding water at the surface. The right choice under new turf.

Aged Horse Manure ($60/bucket) — sold by the bucket rather than the metre. Well rotted, for digging into vegie beds ahead of planting.

Sands

Fill / House Pad Sand ($55/m³) — the cheap bulk option for raising levels, filling holes and building up a pad. Not screened, not pretty, and doesn't need to be.

Screened Sand ($50/m³) — run through a screen to take the coarse material out. General-purpose and the best value per metre on the board.

Brickies Sand ($80/m³) — the orange-yellow sand mortar is mixed from. Consistent grade, which is what matters when you're trying to keep mortar colour even across a wall.

Grit Sand ($85/m³) — coarser and sharper. Used for rendering, screed beds and anywhere you want drainage rather than water retention.

Plasterers Sand ($130/m³) — fine and clean, for plaster and render coats where a coarse grain would show in the finish.

Playground Sand ($130/m³) — washed and screened so it's clean enough for kids to dig in. Soft underfoot and free of the sharp material you'd find in a fill sand.
