How much do I need?

Coverage and depth

Mulch is sold by the cubic metre, and how far it goes depends entirely on how deep you spread it. For most garden beds around Margaret River, 75 mm is the number worth aiming at — deep enough to actually suppress weeds and hold moisture through a dry February, shallow enough not to waste material.

At 75 mm13 m²per cubic metre — the standard for a new garden bed
At 50 mm20 m²per cubic metre — enough for topping up an established bed
At 100 mm10 m²per cubic metre — for serious weed suppression on bare ground
A trailer load~73 m²5.5 m³ spread at 75 mm — most suburban front gardens

Anything under about 50 mm and weeds push straight through, so you end up doing the job twice. If you're mulching over bare soil for the first time, go to 100 mm and it'll still be working next summer.

Which one

Choosing a mulch

Marri Chips ($150/m³) — coarse, pale local marri chip. The longest-lasting option we carry and the best choice for windy blocks: the chunky grade doesn't blow around the way fine mulch does. Good under established natives and along driveways.

Pine Bark ($150/m³) — chunky red-brown bark, the most decorative of the range. Holds its colour well and sits neatly in front-garden beds. Slower to break down than a shredded mulch, so it's more about looks and less about feeding the soil.

Bushland ($130/m³) — coarse mixed brown wood chip and our most popular all-rounder. Natural look, honest price, does the job across big areas without shouting for attention.

Woodland Brown or Black ($130/m³) — coloured chip for when you want a defined, landscaped edge. The black in particular makes green foliage pop, though on a hot exposed bed it will absorb more heat than a natural mulch.

Karri & Peat ($130/m³) — fine, dark and rich. Breaks down faster than the chunky grades, which means it actively improves the soil underneath. Best on garden beds you're building up rather than paths or slopes.

Derby Brown ($130/m³) — fine dark brown shredded mulch. Knits together as it settles, so it stays put on a gentle slope better than a loose chip will.

Plantation ($110/m³) — the budget option, and genuinely the right call when you're covering a large area and the look matters less than the coverage. Firebreaks, back paddocks, orchard rows.

Natural products vary a little batch to batch — the photos below are real yard shots with a hand in frame so you can judge the grade.
